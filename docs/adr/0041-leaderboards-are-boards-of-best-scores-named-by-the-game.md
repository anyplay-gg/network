# Leaderboards are boards of best scores, named by what the game passes

Games on the platform rank their players: a time trial, a high score, a weekly challenge. The game records a score for a board and later reads a range of it. Both are online calls of the platform, so AnyPlay answers them for AnyPlay accounts ([ADR 0021](0021-the-line-is-drawn-at-the-law.md)); nothing is sent to a platform's servers, and no publisher's own ranking service is replaced. This is the first of the platform services after sessions that [the roadmap](../DESIGN.md#roadmap) lists as "More".

## Decision

- **A board is a title and what the game passes.** The network keys a board by the title ID of the running game start and a **board ID**: a text of 1 to 64 characters from `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -`. The engine writes the game's own board number or name into that text and never interprets it. No title ID and no board ID appears in network or cloud code; the data (which boards exist, which end is better, which scores are plausible) is per-board rows in the cloud, set by moderators.
- **An entry is the best score of one account on one board.** A submitted score replaces the account's entry only when it is better by the board's order; an equal score keeps the earlier time. The cloud keeps no history of scores, only the best and when it was reached. A score is a signed 64-bit integer; times, points and distances are the game's to scale into one.
- **The board knows its order and bounds.** Each board has an order (`high` or `low` is better, `high` for a new board), a lowest and a highest score it takes (0 and 2^53 - 1 for a new board) and a status (`open` or `closed`). The bridge cannot know the order, because the platform's library reads it from the publisher's setup, not from the call. So a board is made on the first accepted score with the defaults, and a moderator sets the real order and bounds from observation. Until then a `low` board ranks wrong and the answer says so in nothing but its order: the data fixes it, not a release.
- **A board has a name for the game only.** Names, art and descriptions of boards stay in the game's files, as trophy texts do ([ADR 0009](0009-trophy-records-not-trophy-texts.md)). The web page shows `Board 7`, a launcher shows what its own copy of the game says.
- **Three views, one rank rule.** A viewer reads the **top** (the best entries they may see), the entries **around** their own, or **friends** (themselves and their friends). Rank is the place among the entries the viewer may see in that view, and equal scores share a rank (1, 2, 2, 4). Ordering inside a tie is earlier time first, then account ID, so a page is stable.
- **Scores are shown to friends unless the player says otherwise.** A new setting `scores` has the three audiences of the profile parts (friends by default, everyone, nobody) and follows [ADR 0012](0012-private-by-default.md): a stranger sees an entry only if its owner chose `everyone`, a friend sees it unless the owner chose `nobody`, a block in either direction hides it both ways, and the owner sees their own entry always. A hidden entry is not a gap: ranks are counted among what the viewer may see, so no rank reveals a hidden player.
- **The setting is a call of its own,** `PUT /v1/me/score-privacy`, like [appearing offline](0033-appearing-offline-hides-presence-from-everyone.md) and "remember people I play with" ([ADR 0038](0038-played-with-recently-is-a-private-list-the-cloud-keeps-for-30-days.md)): `PUT /v1/me/privacy` replaces all audiences with defaults for what is left out, and the privacy object is built by struct literal in clients that would stop compiling with a new field. It can join `Privacy` when no client depends on the shape.
- **Anti-abuse is data and limits, not detection.**
  - A cloud takes 60 submissions per account per 10 minutes, reads at the usual account limit, and the public reads on the web at the public-profile limit.
  - A title has at most 64 boards, an account may make at most 16 boards and keeps a score on at most 1,000. A game cannot flood the cloud with made-up boards, and an account that is not playing a title can make no more than 16 junk boards in all.
  - A score outside the bounds of its board is refused, not clamped.
  - A moderator removes an entry with a reason. The removal stays as a mark on the row, so the account cannot put a score back by submitting again, until a moderator restores it. A moderator can close a board, which keeps its entries and takes no more.
  - Banned accounts are left out of every view; their rows stay for the length of the ban's retention and go with the account.
  - No cheat detection and no check of the game: a score is what the engine reports, and the network neither inspects nor repairs a game's anti-cheat, in either direction.
- **Off by default.** The feature is behind `ANYPLAY_CLOUD_LEADERBOARDS_ENABLED`. Off, every route answers `forbidden` and the web reads `not_found`. A public cloud turns it on when the privacy notice names scores ([ADR 0021](0021-the-line-is-drawn-at-the-law.md) asks for review before answering platform calls publicly).
- **Protocol 1 stays compatible.** Four new message kinds in a group of their own, `0x06xx`: record a score and read a range, each a request with a reply. An older net service answers `unsupported` and the bridge treats that as the platform's "service not available" ([spec](../../spec/bridge-protocol.md#leaderboards-0x06xx)). The bridge never sends a title: the net service knows it from the game start.

## Considered options

- **History of every score.** Lets a client draw a progress curve and recount a board after removing a cheat. It also stores every attempt of every player, which grows without bound and records a player's whole play. The best score and its time are what a board shows.
- **Time-boxed boards (daily, weekly, seasons).** Games that want a weekly board pass a board ID that changes (`weekly-43`), so a board is already time-boxed by its name, and the cloud needs no calendar of its own. Resets the cloud runs itself would need a rule per game.
- **Taking the order from the first score or from the game.** Neither is knowable: a first score says nothing about whether lower is better. The platform's library takes it from the publisher's configuration, which we do not have and do not copy.
- **Global top that shows everyone regardless of privacy.** Fills a new board at once and makes every launcher a place where names are listed without being asked. [ADR 0012](0012-private-by-default.md) decided against it. A board of friends is full from the first game; the public top fills as players choose to appear.
- **One rank for everyone (counting hidden players).** A stable number, but a hidden player's score would show as a gap in the ranks and could be located by anyone willing to count. A rank among what the viewer may see costs a window function per read and leaks nothing.
- **Anti-cheat of our own (replay checks, statistical outliers).** An arms race against games we do not control, and a wrong ban costs more than a wrong score. Bounds, rate limits and removal by a moderator are the whole of it.
- **A new `Part` in `Privacy`.** Shorter in the settings page, but see "A setting of its own" above.

## Consequences

- A new section in the [cloud API](../../spec/cloud-api.md#leaderboards) and the [portal API](../../spec/portal-api.md#leaderboards), the bridge group, and a section in [the design](../DESIGN.md#leaderboards).
- One more table family in the cloud's data inventory (boards, scores, the `scores` audience), deleted with the account and part of its export; one more line in the privacy notice before the flag goes on.
- The net service needs a handler for the two requests; the engine needs the library calls ([follow-up for the engine owners](../DESIGN.md#leaderboards)).
- A score reported by a game is trusted as much as the game: a modified client can post any score within the bounds. Boards are a convenience between players, not a competition; a tournament would need more and is not planned.
