# Share play streams through a worker, on a connection of its own

Share play sends the host's game to one friend in their party as video and sound, and brings the friend's pad back. Five choices carry it. The [spec](../../spec/share-play.md) has the details and the [plan](../share-play-plan.md) the order of work.

**The engine copies and injects, nothing more.** AnyPS5 hands the presented picture and the mixed game sound to a shared-memory ring, and takes validated pad states from the bridge. It does not encode, packetise or send. A separate program, the **share worker**, reads the ring, encodes, cuts the result into datagrams and adds loss protection. On the guest's PC the same program decodes and shows the picture. The net service only forwards the worker's datagrams. This keeps the engine change to a copy, a mix and a pad source; keeps driver code that may crash (hardware encoders and decoders) out of the engine and out of the process that holds the account and the tunnels; and keeps codec libraries and their licences out of both. The worker is downloaded by the launcher on first use, which the terms of the one royalty-covered software H.264 encoder we may use also ask for.

**Share play has a connection of its own.** The net services of host and guest hold a third QUIC connection on the tunnels' iroh endpoint, with the ALPN `anyplay/share/0`, for the same reasons as voice ([ADR 0017](0017-voice-has-a-connection-of-its-own.md)): relay-only, hole punching, relay tickets and the member checks apply with no code of their own. Video, audio and pad states travel as QUIC datagrams, which QUIC's congestion controller already governs ([RFC 9221](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9221)). Control messages go on one stream. Only the guest the cloud's share names can connect.

**Codecs: AV1 when both ends can, H.264 otherwise, never HEVC.** AV1 is covered by the Alliance for Open Media's royalty-free licence, although one patent pool disputes that. H.264 is the codec every PC can decode in hardware. HEVC sits in several competing patent pools and adds nothing we need. Encoding uses the GPU vendors' own runtimes, loaded from the driver, never shipped by us. Software encoding is OpenH264 as a separate download from Cisco, never x264, which is GPL. The hardware route does not remove the patent question, and the spec says so.

**The guest's input is a pad, never the host's operating system.** The guest sends pad states from a fixed list of buttons, sticks and triggers. The host's net service checks and rate-limits them; the engine's pad layer receives them as a pad. Nothing is injected into the operating system, so a guest can never reach the desktop, a keyboard or another program. A pad chord on the host's own pad, a key and a button in the launcher end or reduce the share at once.

**Both sides agree every time, and only inside a party.** The host invites one party member, the member accepts, the host picks a mode, and the host alone can change it. Nothing is remembered between shares. Until a legal review is done, the cloud reports share play as off ([ADR 0008](0008-the-online-permission-check-waits.md) waits the same way), because a guest without the game plays through the host's copy, which the [clean line](0003-the-clean-line.md) asks us to look at with care.

## Considered options

- **WebRTC:** a full stack for this job, but a second stack beside iroh with its own relays and IP rules, rejected for voice in [ADR 0010](0010-voice-and-shared-play-go-through-the-tunnels.md) and for the same reasons here.
- **Encode inside the engine** (Vulkan Video, or the ffmpeg build it already carries for game movies): zero-copy in the best case, but the engine grows by an encoder, the driver coverage of Vulkan encode is uneven, and every codec bug becomes a crash of the game.
- **Encode inside the net service:** one process less, but a driver fault would end the player's tunnels and voice, and codec libraries would enter a program that is meant to stay small.
- **Capture the desktop and inject input into the operating system, as Parsec and Steam Remote Play do:** works for any program, but the guest sees everything on the host's screen, and a guest's input can reach everything on the host's PC. The engine already knows its own picture and its own pad.
- **Moonlight or Sunshine:** they are GPL, they speak another company's protocol, and we take ideas only.
- **HEVC:** better than H.264 per bit, but three pools with different terms. AV1 gives the gain without the pools, at the cost of fewer hardware encoders today.
- **One QUIC stream per frame, reset when late:** retransmission inside a frame's deadline and no loss protection of our own, the choice of Media over QUIC. It is the fallback if the first measurements show that datagrams with our loss protection do not hold up over a relay ([plan, phase 0](../share-play-plan.md#phase-0-a-prototype-without-the-engine)).
- **A tag byte in the tunnel's datagrams or a second endpoint:** rejected in ADR 0017.
